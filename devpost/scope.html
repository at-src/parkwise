ParkWise

A browser-based, interactive 3D parking simulator where a learner driver actually drives the car to practice real parking maneuvers — with physics-based movement and a coach that gives real spatial guidance.

● Scope approved

The Unique Kernel

It's realistic, not arcade. Unlike the many pay-store arcade parking games, ParkWise models the car with real physics (not random or faked motion) and includes a coach that gives genuine spatial guidance — how far the car ahead is, how much room is left behind, how far to reverse and adjust — alongside proper reference lines. Real physics + a real coach is the thing a learner would miss if it were gone.

Who It's For

The learner's cousin: a learner driver preparing for her test who is anxious about parking and doesn't yet have a good grip on the maneuvers. Today she can only practice in a real car (stressful, limited chances) or play arcade games that don't behave like a real car. She wants a low-pressure place to practice until the maneuvers feel natural. Broader learner drivers are a welcome bonus.

The Core Loop

She opens the link in a browser — nothing to install — and is in a parking scenario straight away. She drives the car herself (steer, forward, reverse). The coach gives live spatial guidance (distance to the car ahead/behind, how much to reverse, when to straighten) and can also demonstrate the maneuver itself — a "watch how it's done" tutorial mode. She sees whether she parked cleanly within the lines, then resets and retries until it feels natural.

Why This Matters

"Last week I met my cousin who has her driving test and she was anxious that she didn't have good grip on parking situations."

The learner overcame the same difficulty through a lot of practice and wants to give her a place to build that confidence. It's also a chance to get better at Three.js and at writing code grounded in real-world physics.

What "Working" Looks Like

She opens the link, lands in a scenario, and drives the car. The physics make it move like a real car. The coach reports real distances and tells her how much to adjust. She completes a maneuver, gets a clear "parked correctly / hit the curb" verdict, and can reset to retry.

The "oh, that's cool" beat: reversing into a parallel spot in 3D while the coach counts down the distance to the car behind — and nailing it.

The Boundary

In — the proof of concept ›
  • Interactive 3D scene in the browser (Three.js), drivable car.
  • Physics-based car movement (steering, forward/reverse) that behaves believably.
  • Common parking scenarios — parallel, reverse bay, and angle parking as the core set; more from the learner's own experience if time permits.
  • A coach that gives live spatial guidance with reference lines and can demonstrate the maneuver (tutorial mode).
  • A clear success/fail check against the parking lines.
  • Reset / try again.
  • Proximity sensor sound cues (beeps that speed up near obstacles), likely Web Audio API — included if it fits; approach decided at build.
Later — worth doing, not now ›
  • Further parking scenarios beyond the core set, added from the learner's own experience as time allows.
  • Deployment to a public URL (optional for the hackathon).
  • Scoring, progress tracking, or lessons/levels.
Explicitly cut — with reasons ›
  • Open world / free driving — expands scope well beyond parking practice; the point is the maneuvers.
  • AI or moving traffic — significant scope increase (behavior, pathfinding) not needed to prove the kernel; static parked cars as obstacles are enough.
